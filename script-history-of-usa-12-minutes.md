---

## 1. BEFORE THE SETTLERS, AND EUROPEAN ARRIVAL (0:35–1:35)

[VISUAL: map of Native nations: Cherokee, Sioux, Iroquois, Navajo, Pueblo; Columbus's ships]

"Long before the United States existed, this land was home to millions of people and hundreds of distinct nations. From the great city of Cahokia, near modern St. Louis, to the powerful Iroquois Confederacy in the Northeast.

In 1492, Christopher Columbus sails across the Atlantic looking for a route to Asia and lands in the Caribbean. A wave of explorers and conquistadors follows. The Spanish settle Florida and the Southwest. St. Augustine, founded in 1565, is the oldest European-founded city in the country.

The English arrive later. In 1607 they found Jamestown, Virginia. In 1620, the Mayflower brings the Pilgrims to Massachusetts.

But European arrival brought devastation: diseases like smallpox, against which Native peoples had no immunity. Indigenous populations collapsed within just a few generations."

---

## 2. THE THIRTEEN COLONIES AND SLAVERY (1:35–2:35)

[VISUAL: map of the 13 colonies; slave ship; tobacco plantations]

"Through the 1600s and 1700s, England builds thirteen colonies along the East Coast. Each had its own character: in the North, trade and small farms; in the South, huge plantations of tobacco, rice, and cotton.

And those plantations ran on enslaved labor. In 1619, the first Africans arrive in Virginia, and over time millions would be kidnapped and brought by force. Slavery would become the country's greatest wound, and its greatest conflict, for the next two centuries."

---

## 3. REVOLUTION AND INDEPENDENCE (2:35–4:00)

[VISUAL: Boston Tea Party, Declaration of Independence, Washington crossing the Delaware]

"After winning the war against France in 1763, Britain is deeply in debt, and decides to tax the colonies. The colonists protest with a phrase that became famous: 'No taxation without representation.'

Tensions rise: the Boston Massacre in 1770, the Boston Tea Party in 1773, and in 1775 war breaks out at Lexington and Concord.

On July 4th, 1776, the Declaration of Independence is adopted, written mainly by Thomas Jefferson. It says 'all men are created equal'... though at the time, that left out enslaved people, Native Americans, and women.

With George Washington in command and crucial help from France, the colonists win at Yorktown in 1781. Britain recognizes independence in 1783.

In 1787 the Constitution is written, and it's still in force today. In 1789, Washington becomes the first president."

---

## 4. WESTWARD EXPANSION (4:00–5:15)

[VISUAL: animated map of the territory growing; wagon trains; Lewis and Clark]

"The new country was small: barely the East Coast. But it grows fast.

In 1803, the U.S. buys the huge Louisiana Territory from France for 15 million dollars, doubling its size overnight. Lewis and Clark set out to explore the West.

The idea of 'Manifest Destiny' takes hold: the belief that the nation was destined to stretch from coast to coast. In 1846, war with Mexico begins, and in 1848 Mexico cedes California, Nevada, Utah, and much of the Southwest.

In 1849, the Gold Rush draws hundreds of thousands of people to California.

And all the while, expansion came at a brutal cost for Native peoples: the Indian Removal Act of 1830 and the Trail of Tears drove tens of thousands from their homelands."

---

## 5. THE CIVIL WAR (5:15–6:40)

[VISUAL: Lincoln, Battle of Gettysburg, North vs. South map]

"By the mid-1800s, the fight over whether new territories would be free or slave divides the nation. In 1860, Abraham Lincoln, who opposed the spread of slavery, is elected president.

Seven Southern states break away and form the Confederacy. In April 1861, the Civil War begins.

It was the bloodiest conflict in American history: about 620,000 soldiers died. In 1863, Lincoln issues the Emancipation Proclamation and delivers the Gettysburg Address. In 1865, the South surrenders. That same year, the 13th Amendment abolishes slavery. But Lincoln is assassinated just days after the surrender.

Then came Reconstruction, an attempt to bring Black Americans into full citizenship. But when it ended, the South imposed 'Jim Crow' segregation laws that would last nearly a century."

---

## 6. INDUSTRIALIZATION AND RISING POWER (6:40–7:50)

[VISUAL: transcontinental railroad, Ellis Island, skyscrapers, Edison, Rockefeller]

"After the war, the country transforms. In 1869, the transcontinental railroad is completed. Electricity, the telephone, steel, and oil take off. Names like Rockefeller, Carnegie, and Edison dominate an era known as the Gilded Age.

Millions of immigrants arrive from Europe, passing through places like Ellis Island, in view of the Statue of Liberty, dedicated in 1886.

In 1898, after the war with Spain, the U.S. gains Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines, and emerges as a power with interests far beyond its own continent."

---

## 7. WORLD WARS AND THE GREAT DEPRESSION (7:50–9:05)

[VISUAL: soldiers in 1917, Wall Street 1929, unemployment lines, Pearl Harbor, Hiroshima]

"The U.S. enters World War I in 1917, and its help tips the balance. The 1920s are years of parties and prosperity... until October 1929, when the New York stock market crashes.

The Great Depression begins: a quarter of workers lose their jobs. President Franklin D. Roosevelt responds with the New Deal, a massive package of reforms and public works.

On December 7th, 1941, Japan attacks Pearl Harbor, and the U.S. enters World War II. Its industry becomes 'the arsenal of democracy.' The war ends in 1945 after atomic bombs are dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

And the United States emerges as the greatest economic and military power on the planet."

---

## 8. THE COLD WAR AND CIVIL RIGHTS (9:05–10:20)

[VISUAL: missiles, Moon landing, Martin Luther King Jr., Rosa Parks]

"The Cold War begins: a decades-long standoff with the Soviet Union. There was no direct war between the two, but there were conflicts like Korea and Vietnam, the Cuban Missile Crisis, and the Space Race, which peaks when Neil Armstrong walks on the Moon in 1969.

At home, the Civil Rights Movement erupts. Rosa Parks, Martin Luther King Jr., and thousands of others fight segregation. The Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965 are passed.

The 1970s bring the Watergate scandal, which forces Richard Nixon to resign, and oil crises. In the 1980s, Ronald Reagan pushes tax cuts and a hard line against Moscow. In 1991, the Soviet Union dissolves."

---

## 9. THE LONE SUPERPOWER AND THE 21ST CENTURY (10:20–11:35)

[VISUAL: Silicon Valley, 9/11, Obama, 2008 crisis, pandemic]

"In the 1990s, the U.S. stands as the world's only superpower. The internet and Silicon Valley supercharge the economy.

But on September 11th, 2001, terrorist attacks kill nearly 3,000 people. The wars in Afghanistan and Iraq follow, lasting for years.

In 2008, a global financial crisis rocks the economy. That year, Barack Obama is elected, the first Black president. Then come Donald Trump's first term in 2017, Joe Biden in 2021, and the COVID-19 pandemic, which killed more than a million Americans.

In 2024, Trump wins again and returns to the White House in 2025. Today, the country faces deep political polarization, fierce debates over immigration, tariffs, and artificial intelligence, and growing competition with China."

---
