## HOOK (0:00–0:25)

[VISUAL: world map in 1491, zoom into the Americas; cut to modern New York]

"Five hundred years ago, almost nobody in Europe knew it existed. Today, its economy produces over 28 trillion dollars a year.

This is the story of the United States: wars, revolutions, slavery, inventions, and reinventions. All in nine minutes. Let's go."

---

## 1. BEFORE THE SETTLERS (0:25–1:10)

[VISUAL: map of Native nations; Columbus's ships; Mayflower]

"Long before the United States, this land was home to millions of people and hundreds of nations, from the city of Cahokia to the powerful Iroquois Confederacy.

In 1492, Columbus lands in the Caribbean, and explorers follow. The Spanish found St. Augustine in 1565. The English found Jamestown in 1607, and the Pilgrims arrive on the Mayflower in 1620.

But European arrival brought diseases like smallpox, and Native populations collapsed within a few generations."

---

## 2. THE THIRTEEN COLONIES AND SLAVERY (1:10–1:50)

[VISUAL: map of the 13 colonies; slave ship; plantations]

"By the 1700s, England has thirteen colonies on the East Coast: trade and small farms in the North, giant plantations in the South.

Those plantations ran on enslaved labor. The first Africans arrive in Virginia in 1619, and over time millions would be brought by force. Slavery would become the country's greatest wound for the next two centuries."

---

## 3. INDEPENDENCE (1:50–3:00)

[VISUAL: Boston Tea Party, Declaration of Independence, Washington crossing the Delaware]

"After the war with France, Britain is in debt and taxes the colonies. The colonists answer: 'No taxation without representation.'

Tensions explode: the Boston Tea Party in 1773, and war in 1775.

On July 4th, 1776, the Declaration of Independence says 'all men are created equal'... though that left out enslaved people, Native Americans, and women.

With George Washington leading and help from France, the colonists win at Yorktown in 1781. In 1787 the Constitution is written, and in 1789 Washington becomes the first president."

---

## 4. WESTWARD EXPANSION (3:00–3:50)

[VISUAL: animated map of the territory growing; wagon trains]

"The new country was small, but it grows fast. In 1803, the U.S. buys Louisiana from France for 15 million dollars, doubling its size overnight.

'Manifest Destiny', the belief the nation should stretch coast to coast, drives expansion. After the war with Mexico, in 1848, the U.S. gains California and much of the Southwest. In 1849, the Gold Rush pulls hundreds of thousands westward.

For Native peoples, it was brutal: the Indian Removal Act and the Trail of Tears forced tens of thousands from their lands."

---

## 5. THE CIVIL WAR (3:50–4:50)

[VISUAL: Lincoln, Gettysburg, North vs. South map]

"By the mid-1800s, the fight over slavery tears the country apart. In 1860, Abraham Lincoln is elected, and seven Southern states break away to form the Confederacy. In 1861, the Civil War begins.

It was the bloodiest war in American history: about 620,000 soldiers died. Lincoln issues the Emancipation Proclamation in 1863, the South surrenders in 1865, and the 13th Amendment abolishes slavery. Days later, Lincoln is assassinated.

Reconstruction tried to bring Black Americans into full citizenship, but it was followed by 'Jim Crow' segregation laws that lasted almost a century."

---

## 6. INDUSTRY AND RISING POWER (4:50–5:30)

[VISUAL: railroad, Ellis Island, Statue of Liberty, Edison, Rockefeller]

"After the war, the country transforms. The transcontinental railroad is completed in 1869. Electricity, steel, and oil take off, led by names like Edison, Carnegie, and Rockefeller.

Millions of immigrants arrive through Ellis Island, past the Statue of Liberty. And after the war with Spain in 1898, the U.S. gains Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines, and becomes a world power."

---

## 7. WORLD WARS AND THE DEPRESSION (5:30–6:30)

[VISUAL: soldiers in 1917, Wall Street 1929, Pearl Harbor, Hiroshima]

"The U.S. enters World War I in 1917 and helps decide it. Then in October 1929, the stock market crashes, and the Great Depression leaves a quarter of workers jobless. Franklin D. Roosevelt answers with the New Deal.

After Japan attacks Pearl Harbor in 1941, America joins World War II and becomes 'the arsenal of democracy.' The war ends in 1945, and the United States emerges as the strongest economic and military power on Earth."

---

## 8. COLD WAR AND CIVIL RIGHTS (6:30–7:35)

[VISUAL: missiles, Moon landing, Martin Luther King Jr., Rosa Parks]

"The Cold War pits the U.S. against the Soviet Union for decades: Korea, Vietnam, the Cuban Missile Crisis, and the Space Race, which peaks when Neil Armstrong walks on the Moon in 1969.

At home, Rosa Parks, Martin Luther King Jr., and thousands of others lead the Civil Rights Movement, winning the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Then comes Watergate, forcing Nixon to resign, and in 1991 the Soviet Union collapses, leaving the U.S. as the lone superpower."

---

## 9. THE 21ST CENTURY (7:35–8:35)

[VISUAL: Silicon Valley, 9/11, Obama, pandemic, current headlines]

"The internet and Silicon Valley supercharge the 1990s economy. But on September 11th, 2001, terrorist attacks kill nearly 3,000 people, leading to long wars in Afghanistan and Iraq.

In 2008, a financial crisis shakes the world, and Barack Obama becomes the first Black president. Then come Trump in 2017, Biden in 2021, and the COVID-19 pandemic, which killed over a million Americans.

Trump wins again in 2024 and returns to office in 2025. Today the country faces deep polarization, debates over immigration, tariffs, and AI, and rising competition with China."

---

## OUTRO (8:35–9:00)

[VISUAL: fast montage of the timeline, ending on the Statue of Liberty]

"From colonies to superpower, with light and shadow. The story of the United States is still being written, and 2026 is just the next chapter.

If you enjoyed this, subscribe, and tell me in the comments: which country should we cover next?"
